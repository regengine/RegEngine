by addressing it immediately — before they raise it, before it
becomes awkward in a discovery call.

## Reply template (paste, swap `{{first_name}}`)

```
Hi {{first_name}} —

Quick honest note before anything else: my original message offered
$100 for the 30 minutes. I have to walk that back — founder budget
is tighter than I assumed when I sent it, and I shouldn't have
promised something I can't reliably honor.

The 30-minute ask still stands, no honorarium, no obligation. If
the value exchange doesn't work for you under those terms, I
completely understand and there's no need to reply.

If you're still up for it, the Calendly link in my original note
goes to a 30-minute slot:
https://calendly.com/regengine/meet-and-greet

Apologies for the back-and-forth. I'd rather start by being
straight with you than have it come up mid-call.

— Christopher
```

## Why this wording

- **Lead with the retraction, before any other content.** Hiding it
  past pleasantries would feel like a bait-and-switch when they
  catch it.
- **"I shouldn't have promised something I can't reliably honor"** —
  own the mistake explicitly. Don't blame budget, market, or
  circumstance. Own the decision.
- **"No honorarium, no obligation"** — sets the floor; any
  continued engagement is purely their choice.
- **"There's no need to reply"** — gives them a graceful out. Some
  will take it. Some will respond with respect for the honesty.
  Either is fine.
- **No re-pitching the product, value prop, or urgency.** The
  follow-up is about character, not conversion. If they book after
  this, the call starts from a place of trust.

## If they book the call before you've sent this

Use the **first 60 seconds of the call** to deliver the same content
verbatim, then ask permission to continue. Don't let the $100 sit
unaddressed through 30 minutes of discovery and surface it at the end.

## What NOT to do

- Don't offer a smaller honorarium ($25, $50) as a compromise — it
  signals you're still trying to buy their time. Either the $100
  stands or the offer is fully retracted.
- Don't offer "12 months free access at launch" as a substitute.
  That was in DM #2 originally; the user direction on
  2026-05-26 was no offer at all. Adding it back via a follow-up
  contradicts the documented retraction.
- Don't apologize more than once. One clear ownership beat. Excess
  apology reads as weak, not character.
