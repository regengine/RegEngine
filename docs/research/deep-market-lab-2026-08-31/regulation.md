# Regulatory evidence and falsification: FSMA 204

Research cutoff and access date: **August 31, 2026**. This is a docs-only regulatory/product assessment, not legal advice, a compliance certification, or evidence of FDA acceptance of RegEngine. Sources are FDA, Federal Register, and eCFR primary materials. No source dated after the cutoff is used; some retrieved eCFR pages disclose earlier August/July currency dates.

Labels: **[FACT]** directly supported external evidence; **[REPO]** inspected repository evidence; **[INFERENCE]** reasoned implication; **[HYPOTHESIS]** untested commercial proposition; **[UNKNOWN]** not verified. Confidence concerns the specific claim, not the whole business.

## Decision this evidence supports

**[INFERENCE, medium confidence]** The rule supports a narrow product that helps covered operators obtain missing supplier information, retain linked records, and assemble a complete response to a scoped records request. It does not establish demand for a separate cryptographic evidence product. The best near-term regulatory experiment is an independently reviewed request-response exercise using real, authorized operator records and the operator's actual exemption/CTE profile. Broader compliance positioning is premature while regulatory citations, completeness scoring, and submission behavior disagree.

**[REPO]** This fits the production spine and ERP-adjacent intent in [PRODUCT_DESCRIPTION.md](../../../docs/PRODUCT_DESCRIPTION.md#L7). That document also states the product is pre-production with no paying customers. No customer commitment or field acceptance was verified in this lane.

## Ground truth that changes the market thesis

**R1 — Deadline pressure is materially weaker than a 2026 enforcement story. [FACT, high]** FDA's current rule page distinguishes the original January 20, 2026 compliance date, a proposed extension to July 20, 2028, and Congress's direction that FDA not enforce before July 20, 2028. FDA says it intends to follow that direction. The extension document remains labeled a proposed rule in the sources checked; a final compliance-date amendment was **not verified**. Use “congressional non-enforcement floor of July 20, 2028; compliance-date extension proposed,” not “the final rule has definitively moved the compliance deadline.” [FDA rule status](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods), [FDA proposed-extension analysis](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/economic-impact-analyses-fda-regulations/requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods-compliance-date-extension-ria-proposed).

**[INFERENCE]** A countdown is insufficient evidence of current budget. Urgency must be verified through a named buyer requirement, recurring labor cost, or a failed drill. Retailer requests may create separate commercial obligations, but no universal earlier retailer deadline is established here.

**R2 — Coverage is conditional, not “every food business.” [FACT, high]** Covered activities are manufacturing, processing, packing, or holding listed foods, including qualifying listed ingredients that remain in their listed form. Full and partial exemptions vary by food, activity, and entity. Examples include certain small farms, direct farm-to-consumer sales, particular processing circumstances, and some retail/restaurant operations. A product category alone cannot determine applicability. [FDA rule scope](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods), [21 CFR 1.1305](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-S/subject-group-ECFRa32e21d2cb23347/section-1.1305).

**[INFERENCE]** Market sizing must deduplicate establishments into buying organizations and exclude or separately price exempt workflows. A questionnaire should classify product form, actual activity, sales basis, exemption evidence, and the party holding records. “FSMA applies” and “customer must deliver a sortable spreadsheet” are separate decisions.

**R3 — The 24-hour obligation has specific scope and exceptions. [FACT, high]** Required records must be available within 24 hours, or another reasonable period agreed by FDA. Electronic sortable spreadsheets are specifically required for applicable requests addressing outbreaks, recalls, or public-health threats. Certain farms below a $250,000 baseline threshold, and qualifying other entities below a $1 million baseline threshold, may provide another format; the three-year measures are inflation-adjusted from 2020. Religious accommodation is also specified. Most covered records require two-year retention. These are not current inflation-adjusted dollar amounts. [21 CFR 1.1455(c)–(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-S/subject-group-ECFRacd5c2e8ea7ccbf/section-1.1455).

**[FACT]** FDA's spreadsheet is illustrative, not a mandatory proprietary layout. **[INFERENCE]** A fast CSV download is therefore neither necessary proof of compliant recordkeeping nor sufficient proof of a complete response. Request scope, required KDEs, understandable coding, permissions, and retrieval under operational conditions all matter. [FDA spreadsheet explanation](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods).

**R4 — TLCs do not require a GTIN prefix. [FACT, high]** FDA permits firm-defined TLC formats. Assignment generally occurs at initial packing, first land-based receiving from a fishing vessel, or transformation. An ordinary shipping event must not create a replacement TLC. Receiving from an exempt entity has a special assignment rule, with retail/restaurant exceptions. A TLC source identifies where the TLC was assigned, not merely the immediate supplier. [FDA TLC guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/traceability-lot-code).

**[INFERENCE]** Preserve the original lot identifier and source context. Treat GTIN/GLN enrichment as interoperability support. Requiring GS1-style identifiers from all suppliers could manufacture remediation work that the regulation does not require.

**R5 — Shipping data is more than the legacy seven-field checklist. [FACT, high]** Section 1.1340(a) requires linked TLC, quantity/UOM, product description, recipient and shipping-location descriptions, shipping date, TLC source description or reference, and reference-document type and number. Paragraph (b) governs sharing the first seven groups with the next recipient; paragraph (c) addresses pre-initial-packing shipments. These distinctions matter to field validation and citations. [21 CFR 1.1340](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-S/subject-group-ECFRbfe98fb65ccc9f7/section-1.1340).

**R6 — EPCIS and cryptographic proofs are optional product choices. [FACT/INFERENCE, high]** FDA expressly says EPCIS is not required for compliance or submissions, even though its internal Product Tracing System uses it. The records rule permits paper, electronic records, and true copies, allows existing and multiple record sets, and exempts records maintained solely for this subpart from Part 11. No hash-chain or Merkle-proof mandate appears in those requirements. This does not imply such controls lack value; their value needs separate evidence. [FDA interoperability FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/frequently-asked-questions-fsma-food-traceability-rule), [21 CFR 1.1455(a), (e)–(g)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-S/subject-group-ECFRacd5c2e8ea7ccbf/section-1.1455).

**R7 — Operational procedures remain necessary. [FACT, high]** A traceability plan covers record procedures and locations, FTL identification, TLC assignment when applicable, and a contact; qualifying growers need farm maps. Plans must reflect current practices, with prior versions retained for two years. Software export functionality does not replace these obligations. [21 CFR 1.1315](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-S/subject-group-ECFRe6c9096adb572d4/section-1.1315).

## Counterevidence and invalidated prior claims

**[FACT]** FDA's 2026 tabletop report involved 15 volunteer companies across six scenarios. Most responded within 24 hours. Data completeness and sharing remained uneven; partner coordination was more decisive than particular technology. The report explicitly warns against generalizing the sample to the industry. **[INFERENCE]** This supports a remediation/workflow hypothesis but directly weakens “most firms cannot export in time” and “advanced infrastructure is necessary.” It provides no market-wide failure rate or willingness-to-pay estimate. [FDA tabletop report](https://www.fda.gov/media/192993/download).

**[FACT]** FDA's May 2026 discussion paper explored additional lot-level flexibilities and said decisions on their scope had not yet been made. **[INFERENCE]** Do not implement draft possibilities as enacted exemptions or assume today's implementation burden is fixed. Such discussions increase market uncertainty without establishing repeal. [FDA flexibility discussion paper](https://www.fda.gov/media/192696/download).

The evidence therefore rejects these prior assumptions: every supply-chain participant must buy software; FDA requires GTIN-based TLCs/EPCIS/hash chains; 80% filled KDEs imply compliance; a downloaded file proves operational readiness; the 2028 enforcement floor is verified as a finalized compliance amendment. It leaves open whether a particular operator needs RegEngine, whether a retailer values its verifier, and whether reduced exception-handling labor produces enough recurring value.

## Cheap, decisive experiments

These are proposed decision gates, not completed experiments or statistically validated thresholds.

1. **Regulatory contract review — one working day.** Have a qualified food-traceability reviewer map one shipping and one receiving example to exact current provisions, including source-location resolution and document type/number. Pass only if each exported required field is understandable and no mandatory obligation is mislabeled as enrichment. One reproducible missing mandatory field falsifies the broad claim that every required export field is covered until corrected.
2. **Operator-controlled drill — two days.** With permission, use one buyer's real supplier files for a narrow product/date request. Compare its ordinary ERP/spreadsheet process with RegEngine, including corrections and full-data authorization. Measure total staff minutes, unresolved KDEs, missing sources, and elapsed response time. If a spreadsheet-only process is equally complete with similar effort, reject an automation premium for that segment; do not count synthetic demos as customer evidence.
3. **Coverage/admissibility challenge — half a day.** Test an exempt supplier, an ordinary non-GTIN lot, a transformed output with two inputs, a redacted export, and an 8-of-9-field shipment missing TLC source. Require separate statements for field coverage, applicability, integrity, and submission readiness. No “ready” outcome may hide a required unresolved field. These are future functional checks, not tests claimed as run.
4. **Purchase-trigger interview — five screened prospects.** Ask for the actual partner requirement and named budget owner, and separately test payment for missing-data cleanup versus cryptographic proof. Proceed only on evidence of a repeated task plus concrete paid-pilot interest. If urgency disappears when the 2028 non-enforcement fact is disclosed, invalidate the current sales thesis rather than strengthening deadline language. No prospect contact was made.
