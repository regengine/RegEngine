# Deep Market Lab — market, competitors, and commercial thesis

Date and source-access date: **2026-08-31**. Lane: market; docs-only analysis. No outreach, enrollment, purchase, or external mutation was performed. This memo recommends experiments; it does not change product direction, pricing, or customer commitments.

**Verdict: RESHAPE AND TEST.** RegEngine's ERP-adjacent evidence workflow is coherent, but “less expensive FSMA spreadsheet software” and “validation that incumbents do not offer” are unsupported wedges. Test whether a specific operator will pay for faster resolution of a recurring data problem that their existing tools and service providers leave unresolved. The strongest initial candidate is a bounded reconciliation and recall-readiness exercise for a fresh-cut processor. That is a hypothesis, not demonstrated demand.

## Distribution, inertia, and realistic whitespace

**VERIFIED FACT:** iFoodDS documentation describes supplier enrollment driven by customer requirements and an out-of-network email-sharing path. That is evidence of an implemented distribution mechanism, without proving universal retailer acceptance. [Enrollment documentation](https://info.ifoodds.com/kb-trace-exchange/how-do-i-enroll-in-the-trace-exchange-program), [trading-partner documentation](https://info.ifoodds.com/kb-trace-exchange/how-to-connect-to-customer-trading-partners).

**HYPOTHESIS:** Incumbent advantage comes from accepted customer handoffs, existing mapped data, trained staff, contracts, and accountability during an incident. An export feature is easy to compare; responsibility for a failed traceability response is harder to transfer. A buyer can like RegEngine and still rationally reject migration. Regional distribution does not, by itself, demonstrate freedom from customer mandates. Replace the May memo's logo-based disqualification rule with questions about actual required output, accepted delivery method, renewal date, and unresolved work.

Possible whitespace is therefore **an observed exception workflow inside a specific operation**: one processor repeatedly spends time reconciling transformed lots across its ERP export and supplier files, and neither an incumbent's support package nor an internal report removes that work. The customer must show the failure and its cost. A second portal is justified only when it reduces total effort and can feed accepted systems without creating another supplier obligation. This is proposed discovery scope, not a recommendation to build more integrations now.

## Product-thesis tournament

All scores below are qualitative hypotheses. “Fit” means fit with repository positioning, not verified production readiness.

| Thesis | Strongest argument | Falsifier | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad low-cost FSMA suite | Regulatory need and a clear category | Existing entry prices, implementation labor, and feature overlap erase the cost advantage | **Reject as default positioning.** |
| Seafood importer / first receiver specialist | Concrete documents and handoffs | Qualified operators already solve the actual task with Trace Register or iFoodDS; assumed CTE role is wrong | **Park** until a specific uncovered workflow is demonstrated. |
| Fresh-cut transformation reconciliation | Fits intake → normalize → validate → evidence → export and can be shown with actual files | Real records expose no recurring reconciliation pain, or customers cannot supply required inputs | **Test first**, with one facility and one flow. |
| Neutral export verifier / evidence API | Portable verification can reduce dependence on a vendor | Buyers see integrity verification as expected plumbing and will not pay separately | **Supporting trust feature**, not an independent business thesis yet. |
| Consultant-assisted readiness exercise | Can produce learning and a bounded outcome before full rollout | Customers value only one-time consulting, or founder effort cannot repeat economically | **Discovery offer**, with explicit service hours and no assumed SaaS conversion. |

The first two are not “killed markets”; they are weak default entry strategies for this repository. No market-size multiplication rescues an unverified buying trigger. Build a bottom-up opportunity list only after confirming role, covered workflow, current tooling, operational pain, and ability to purchase. Directory membership is not qualification.

## Imitation war game and failed-platform lessons

**HYPOTHESIS — competitor response:** If RegEngine markets fast CSV-to-export, established vendors can emphasize their existing import/export paths. If it markets validation, ReposiTrak can show its advertised checks and staffed correction. If it sells onboarding, iFoodDS can point to included readiness guidance. If it markets hashes, incumbents can add checksums or partner verification. No reviewed evidence supports exclusive technology, privileged data, or a protected distribution route.

The defensible asset would be earned: repeatable mappings for a narrow operational context, trustworthy handling of ugly exceptions, a measured reduction in operator time, references, and an economical route to similar buyers. A mapping library built from unauthorized customer data would create risk, not defensibility. Use permissioned, de-identified fixtures; keep record ownership and egress clear. Retention should follow ongoing operational value, not deliberate lock-in.

**VERIFIED FACT:** Maersk announced TradeLens discontinuation in November 2022; its stated explanation was insufficient industry collaboration and commercial viability despite a viable platform. This is a maritime-trade analogy, **not a failed FSMA competitor**. The lesson is limited: technical feasibility and network ambition do not establish paying coordination. [Maersk announcement](https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2022/11/29/maersk-and-ibm-to-discontinue-tradelens).

## Buyer and pricing experiments

These experiments are recommendations only; outreach requires separate user authorization.

1. **Observe the existing response.** Recruit five qualified fresh-cut operators through existing relationships or a relevant ERP/food-safety adviser. Ask for the last actual trace/retailer data request and a redacted sample, before showing a product. Count completed walkthroughs and accessible data, not directory names or positive comments. Proceed only if at least three independently expose a recurring, material reconciliation task.
2. **Run a bounded comparison.** For two consenting operators, replay one facility's receiving → transformation → shipping flow using their current method and RegEngine. Measure operator minutes, founder minutes, unresolved exceptions, completeness against source records, and ability to rerun after corrections. A working export that hides missing data fails. Proposed gate: halve operator effort with no silent omissions and a reusable mapping on the second dataset. The threshold is an experimental decision rule, not a forecast.
3. **Test willingness to purchase.** Present a written, fixed-scope paid exercise with deliverables, data-handling terms, support hours, and an exit copy of records. An example **hypothesis** is $500 for one rehearsal, compared with the customer's own alternative; this is not a published price change. Record who approves, what budget funds it, and whether they actually commit. Free 12-month access tests willingness to accept free support, not willingness to pay.
4. **Test an adviser channel manually.** Ask one adviser, after authorization, to bring two similar cases without granting reseller exclusivity or building partner infrastructure. If every case needs a different product, stop and narrow. A channel is evidenced by delivered qualified cases and repeatable economics, not a partnership logo.

Do not retain event billing merely because events are easy to count. Compare facility/workflow subscription, bounded implementation fees, and service-only economics after measuring workload. Seasonal bursts and transformation fan-out can make event caps hard for buyers to predict. Conversely, unlimited founder support can destroy a nominal subscription margin. The existing documents' free-access and lifetime-discount promises need one approved commercial policy; this analysis does not override commitments already made.

## Decision and handoff

Proceed with a narrow paid-learning test; do not claim product-market fit or budget a broad launch. If qualified operators cannot show recurring pain, cannot provide inputs, or prefer an existing bundled solution after a fair comparison, stop this wedge rather than add dashboards. If customers buy only the rehearsal, acknowledge a services result until repeat usage proves otherwise.
